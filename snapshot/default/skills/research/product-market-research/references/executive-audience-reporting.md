# Executive-Audience Reporting

Use this reference when a technical comparison, audit, or research workbook must be adapted for a directorate or executive team.

## Produce a separate executive artifact

Do not merely shorten the technical workbook. Rebuild the information hierarchy around the decision:

1. Put the decision, business impact, and recommended action on the first sheet.
2. Follow with the few facts that materially support the decision.
3. Translate technical weaknesses into risk, consequence, action, and completion criterion.
4. Add a concise 30-day action plan when readiness gaps are fixable.
5. Keep methodology, uncertainty, and sources available after the decision pages.

## Language

- Use clear business Russian for management-facing delivery.
- Replace unnecessary jargon and Anglicisms with natural Russian terms.
- Retain established technical terms such as API, MCP, Codex, sandbox, or model IDs when translation would reduce precision or recognizability.
- Explain retained terms once when the audience may not know them.
- Prefer “practical parity” over decimal scoring when the evidence cannot support a meaningful difference.

## Content discipline

- Lead with one recommendation, not a catalogue of alternatives.
- Separate verified facts from interpretation and recommendation.
- Do not present provider marketing or unmatched model benchmarks as proof of superiority.
- Include migration cost, accumulated context, existing procedures, security, support burden, and total cost of ownership in the decision.
- Keep detailed technical matrices in the technical version; the executive version should show only decision-relevant comparisons.

## Verification

- Reopen the executive workbook in a real spreadsheet application.
- Confirm the first sheet is readable without zooming into a dense technical table.
- Verify links, table ranges, risk priorities, action owners or criteria, and artifact integrity.
